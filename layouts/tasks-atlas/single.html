How to read the atlas

Small multiples: one snippet, one change. Every cell is drawn at the same size, so reading across a row shows exactly what changes.

In snippets, · marks a space, → a tab, and ␊ and ␍␊ the line endings. Lines marked − and + show a rewrite, as a diff.

Each row is one aspect of one kind of line: a task’s bullet, a metadata line’s placement, the archive separator’s shape. Each column is a verdict. {{ index $icons "valid" | safeHTML }}Canonical is what a canonical writer emits. {{ index $icons "tolerated" | safeHTML }}Tolerated is accepted, but isn’t the canonical or recommended form. {{ index $icons "invalid" | safeHTML }}Invalid breaks a rule of {{ $spec.Params.version }}. Under each cell are the rules it rests on.

A tolerant reader may still accept invalid input, as long as it avoids destructive rewrites (T-55). A line that starts like a heading, task, metadata line or separator but doesn’t fit that shape is read here as invalid, not as a note, because note lines don’t begin with that syntax (T-13).

The checker follows the spec’s own example where the two disagree, and reports those lines as open rather than invalid. It runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Type or paste a Plaintext Tasks file. Each line gets a type and a verdict, each problem links to the atlas cell it matches, and that cell is outlined above with the line numbers from your file.
